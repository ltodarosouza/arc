# Arc — content provenance and reuse policy

## Purpose and boundary

This policy keeps the question bank traceable and conservative. It is an operational review process, not legal advice. When reuse rights are uncertain, Arc does not publish the item until the owner or a qualified reviewer resolves the question.

In Brazil, copyright protection covers the expression of scientific works, while ideas, methods, and mathematical concepts themselves are not protected in the same way. That means Arc can teach a method or write an original exercise about a concept, but must not copy a protected statement, solution, diagram, or exercise collection merely because the underlying mathematics is familiar. See [Lei 9.610/1998](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9610.htm), especially the distinction between protected works and ideas/methods.

## Source record

Every question has a `question_sources` record with:

- source type: original, open licence, authorised contributor, or other;
- human-readable label and original URL when applicable;
- licence identifier and licence URL when applicable;
- rights holder or contributor identity;
- written-permission reference when permission is the basis for reuse;
- review status, reviewer, and review date.

The source status is one of:

- `unverified`: captured, but not eligible for publication;
- `review_required`: documentation exists but needs a reviewer decision;
- `approved`: compatible rights confirmed for Arc’s intended use;
- `rejected`: may not be published; record the reason internally.

Only `approved` sources can support a published question.

## Allowed starting points

The preferred order is:

1. **Original questions** written for Arc, with a recorded author/contributor agreement.
2. **Written permission** that explicitly covers the intended use, adaptation, and publication scope.
3. **Openly licensed material** whose exact licence and conditions have been checked.
4. **Verified public-domain material**, with the verification basis recorded.

For Creative Commons content, record the exact version and comply with attribution and any share-alike condition. Do not assume that “found online,” “educational,” or “non-commercial” permits reuse. Licences can differ on commercial use and adaptations; review the exact terms. [Creative Commons licence types](https://creativecommons.org/course/cc-cert-edu/unit-3-anatomy-of-a-cc-license/3-3-license-types/)

## Not acceptable without explicit permission

- copied exercises, worked answers, illustrations, scans, or tables from commercial books;
- material from professor PDFs, university platforms, or past exams without a licence or clear permission;
- a close paraphrase that preserves a distinctive protected expression or solution structure;
- copied community submissions whose author and rights are unknown;
- material generated from a protected source in a way that recreates it too closely.

Books and course materials may guide topic coverage and pedagogical level. They must not be treated as a source to reproduce exercises.

## Review checklist

Before approving a source:

1. Identify who owns or grants the relevant rights.
2. Save the exact licence, permission, or public-domain basis.
3. Confirm that Arc’s intended publication and future monetisation are compatible with it.
4. Confirm required attribution, notice, share-alike, or derivative-work obligations.
5. Check that the question and solution do not contain unlicensed copied expression.
6. Record the reviewer and date.

## Attribution and display

When a source requires attribution, show it near the question or solution in a readable form and preserve the source URL and licence. Do not imply that an institution endorses Arc unless that permission is explicit.

## Takedown and correction

If Arc receives a credible rights concern:

1. unpublish the question promptly while it is reviewed;
2. preserve provenance evidence and the review trail;
3. correct attribution, obtain permission, replace with an original item, or permanently reject it;
4. record the decision so the item is not accidentally republished.
